## Coverage taxonomy (5 buckets — cover ALL where applicable)

Allocate queries across these buckets so every bucket gets retrieval signal:

1. **Role, leadership & committees** — current title/seniority (e.g. "Senate Majority Leader," "Ranking Member," "Caucus Chair"), key committee assignments, party leadership roles, term info. This is "who they are" in their institution.
2. **Signature priorities & legislative record** — the issues this rep is known for and any landmark votes / sponsored bills identified with them. Cover the relevant policy sub-areas for this office level (see Sub-area instructions below). NOT a survey of every issue stance.
3. **Donors / campaign finance** — top contributing industries, top PACs, fundraising patterns. Individual donors only if Musk-tier prominence. This isn't OpenSecrets — we don't need a long list of names.
4. **Public statements & press coverage** — speeches, op-eds, interviews, reputable news coverage of their work, response to current news cycles.
5. **Substantive controversies** — ethics complaints, investigations, lawsuits, FEC filings, conduct-in-office issues, formal sanctions. **EXCLUDE** queries that target viral social media moments, AI-generated content drama (deepfakes, AI videos), single-news-cycle gaffes, or book launches — those are noise, not what voters need.

## Sub-area instructions

Before generating queries for each bucket, briefly identify the sub-areas relevant to **this rep's office level**. The goal is a 360° view of what voters care about for this kind of office. Don't shoehorn irrelevant sub-areas — match the breadth of the office.

For bucket 2 (**Signature priorities & legislative record**) specifically — this is the bucket where coverage gaps hurt most:

- **US Senator / House Rep**: sub-areas typically span domestic vs. foreign policy, economic vs. social, plus specific high-salience hot topics (e.g. foreign aid votes, immigration, healthcare, AI/tech, energy). Issue queries covering each so the breadth pool spans their full policy footprint.
- **Governor / State Legislator**: sub-areas typically span budget/taxes, education, healthcare, criminal justice, infrastructure, plus state-specific issues.
- **Mayor / City Council**: sub-areas typically span housing, public safety, transit, schools, sanitation, local budget. **Foreign policy and federal-level issues are NOT relevant — skip them.**

Apply the same office-adaptive thinking to the other buckets where useful (donors: industry / PACs / individuals; controversies: ethics / legal / financial / conduct-in-office; public statements: signature talking points / response to current news cycles).

A city councilor has no foreign policy stance worth surfacing; a US Senator has no city-zoning stance worth surfacing.
